📝 Chapter Notes — Real Numbers

1. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (FTA)

Every composite number can be expressed as a UNIQUE product of prime numbers (ignoring order).
✏️ Example: 12600 = 2³ × 3² × 5² × 7

2. HCF using Prime Factorisation

HCF = Product of COMMON prime factors with LOWEST power
✏️ HCF(1296, 2520): 1296 = 2⁴×3⁴, 2520 = 2³×3²×5×7 → HCF = 2³×3² = 72

3. LCM using Prime Factorisation

LCM = Product of ALL prime factors with HIGHEST power
✏️ LCM(1296, 2520) = 2⁴×3⁴×5×7 = 45360

4. Golden Relation: HCF × LCM = a × b

Valid ONLY for TWO numbers. For three numbers (a,b,c): HCF × LCM ≠ a×b×c

5. Number of Zeros

Zeros in N = Minimum (power of 2, power of 5) in prime factorisation
✏️ N = 2³×3×5⁴×13×19 → Zeros = Min(3,4) = 3

6. Irrational Numbers — Proof Method

Assume rational → a/b form → contradiction → hence irrational ✓
✏️ Key: √2, √3, √5, √p (p prime) are always irrational
✏️ Sum of rational + irrational = ALWAYS irrational
✏️ Product of non-zero rational × irrational = ALWAYS irrational

7. Terminating vs Non-Terminating

p/q TERMINATES ⟺ q = 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ (after simplification, q has ONLY 2s and 5s)
p/q NON-TERMINATING REPEATING ⟺ q has prime factors other than 2 or 5
✏️ 13/3125 = 13/5⁵ → TERMINATES    1/7 → NON-TERMINATING REPEATING

Theorem 1 — FTA

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.

Theorem 2

Prime Divisibility Theorem

Let p be a prime number. If p divides a², then p divides a, where a is a positive integer. This is the KEY to irrationality proofs!

Theorem 3 — Euclid

Euclid's Division Lemma

Given two positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r satisfying a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b.

Exercise 1.2 — Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Q1 Express each number as a product of its prime factors

Express 140, 156, 3825, 5005, 7429 as a product of prime factors.

(a) 140 = 2 × 2 × 5 × 7 = 2² × 5 × 7
(b) 156 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 13 = 2² × 3 × 13
(c) 3825 = 3 × 3 × 5 × 5 × 17 = 3² × 5² × 17
(d) 5005 = 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 = 5 × 7 × 11 × 13
(e) 7429 = 17 × 19 × 23 = 17 × 19 × 23
Q2 Find LCM and HCF and verify LCM × HCF = Product

(a) 26 and 91  |  (b) 510 and 92  |  (c) 336 and 54

(a) 26 and 91
26 = 2 × 13  |  91 = 7 × 13
HCF(26, 91) = 13  |  LCM(26, 91) = 2 × 7 × 13 = 182
Verification: HCF × LCM = 13 × 182 = 2366 = 26 × 91 = 2366 ✓

(b) 510 and 92
510 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 17  |  92 = 2² × 23
HCF = 2  |  LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 × 17 × 23 = 23460
Verification: 2 × 23460 = 46920 = 510 × 92 = 46920 ✓

(c) 336 and 54
336 = 2⁴ × 3 × 7  |  54 = 2 × 3³
HCF = 2 × 3 = 6  |  LCM = 2⁴ × 3³ × 7 = 3024
Verification: 6 × 3024 = 18144 = 336 × 54 = 18144 ✓
✅ All verified!
Q4 Given HCF(306, 657) = 9, find LCM(306, 657)
Using: HCF × LCM = a × b
LCM = (306 × 657) / HCF = (306 × 657) / 9
LCM = 201042 / 9 = 22338
✅ LCM(306, 657) = 22338
Q5 Check whether 6ⁿ can end with digit 0 for any natural number n
6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ
For a number to end with 0, it must be divisible by 10 = 2 × 5
Prime factorisation of 6ⁿ contains 2 and 3, but NOT 5
Since 5 is not a factor of 6ⁿ, it cannot be divisible by 10
✅ 6ⁿ can NEVER end with digit 0
Q6 Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is composite
7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (7 × 11 + 1) = 13 × (77 + 1) = 13 × 78
= 13 × 2 × 39 = 13 × 2 × 3 × 13 = 2 × 3 × 13²
Since it can be expressed as a product of primes with more than one prime factor, it is composite.
✅ Composite number ✓

Exercise 1.3 — Irrational Numbers

Q1 Prove that √5 is irrational
Assume √5 is rational. Let √5 = a/b where a, b are co-prime integers (b ≠ 0)
Squaring: 5 = a²/b² → a² = 5b²
∴ 5 divides a² → 5 divides a → Let a = 5c
Substituting: (5c)² = 5b² → 25c² = 5b² → b² = 5c²
∴ 5 divides b² → 5 divides b
But now 5 divides both a and b — CONTRADICTION (a and b are co-prime)!
✅ ∴ √5 is irrational
Q2 Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational
Assume 3 + 2√5 is rational. Let 3 + 2√5 = a/b (a, b integers, b ≠ 0)
→ 2√5 = a/b – 3 = (a – 3b)/b
→ √5 = (a – 3b)/(2b)
Since a, b are integers, (a – 3b)/(2b) is rational → √5 is rational
But √5 is irrational — CONTRADICTION!
✅ ∴ 3 + 2√5 is irrational
Q3a Prove 1/√2 is irrational
Assume 1/√2 = a/b → √2 = b/a → √2 is rational
But √2 is irrational — CONTRADICTION!
✅ ∴ 1/√2 is irrational

Decimal Expansion Questions

Q1 Without actual division, state whether 13/3125 is terminating or non-terminating
3125 = 5⁵
Since denominator = 5⁵ = 2⁰ × 5⁵ (only factors of 2 and 5)
✅ TERMINATING decimal
Q2 State whether 17/8 terminates or not, and find the decimal expansion
8 = 2³ = 2³ × 5⁰ → denominator has only 2s → TERMINATING
17/8 = 17/(2³) = (17 × 5³)/(2³ × 5³) = 2125/1000
✅ 17/8 = 2.125 (Terminating)

Assertion-Reason Questions

A-R: HCF of two primes p and q is 1 and LCM is p+q — Assertion True or False? CBSE 2024

Assertion (A): For any two prime numbers p and q, their HCF is 1 and LCM is p + q.
Reason (R): For any two natural numbers, HCF × LCM = product of numbers.

A true; R false
✓ A false; R true

A-R: If HCF of two numbers is 16 and product is 3072, then LCM is 162?

Assertion (A): If HCF of two numbers is 16 and their product is 3072, then their LCM is 162.
Reason (R): If p, q are two positive integers, then HCF(p,q) × LCM(p,q) = p × q.

Both A and R true; R explains A
Both true; R doesn't explain A
A true; R false
✓ A false; R true

Prove that √5 is an irrational number. [Q.P. Code 30(B)]
Step 1: Assume √5 is rational. Then √5 = a/b, where a, b are coprime integers and b ≠ 0.
Step 2: Squaring both sides: 5 = a²/b² → a² = 5b²
Step 3: So a² is divisible by 5 → a is also divisible by 5. Let a = 5c.
Step 4: Substituting: (5c)² = 5b² → 25c² = 5b² → b² = 5c²
Step 5: So b² is divisible by 5 → b is also divisible by 5.
Step 6: Both a and b are divisible by 5 — this contradicts that a and b are coprime!
Conclusion: Our assumption is wrong.
✅ Hence, √5 is irrational. [Write "Hence Proved" in board exam]

HCF = a¹b¹ = ab  |  LCM = a³b³
HCF − LCM = ab − a³b³ = ab(1 − a²b²) = ab(1−ab)(1+ab)
✅ Answer: (D) ab(1−ab)(1+ab)
20241 MarkMCQ
(1+√3)² − (1−√3)² is: (A) a positive rational   (B) a negative integer   (C) a positive irrational   (D) a negative irrational
(1+√3)² = 1 + 2√3 + 3 = 4 + 2√3
(1−√3)² = 1 − 2√3 + 3 = 4 − 2√3
Difference = (4+2√3) − (4−2√3) = 4√3
4√3 is positive (√3 > 0) and irrational.
✅ Answer: (C) a positive irrational number
20243 MarksSA
(A) Prove that √2 is an irrational number. [Q.P. Code 30/4/1]
Step 1: Assume √2 is rational. So √2 = a/b, where a, b are coprime integers and b ≠ 0.
Step 2: Squaring: 2 = a²/b² → a² = 2b²
Step 3: a² is even → a is even. Let a = 2c.
Step 4: (2c)² = 2b² → 4c² = 2b² → b² = 2c²
Step 5: b² is even → b is even.
Step 6: Both a and b are even — contradicts that a, b are coprime!
✅ Hence, √2 is irrational. Hence Proved ✓
20243 MarksSA
(B) Let x = x²y³, q = xy⁴, r = x⁵y² (x, y distinct primes). Find HCF and LCM of p, q, r. Verify if HCF × LCM = p × q × r.
p = x²y³  |  q = xy⁴  |  r = x⁵y²
HCF = lowest powers = x¹y² = xy²
LCM = highest powers = x⁵y⁴
HCF × LCM = xy² × x⁵y⁴ = x⁶y⁶
p × q × r = x²y³ × xy⁴ × x⁵y² = x⁸y⁹
x⁶y⁶ ≠ x⁸y⁹ → HCF × LCM ≠ p × q × r (formula valid for 2 numbers only!)
✅ HCF = xy², LCM = x⁵y⁴. HCF×LCM ≠ p×q×r (confirms: formula works for 2 numbers only)

20241 MarkMCQ
(√3 + 2)² + (√3 − 2)² is a/an: (A) positive rational   (B) negative rational   (C) positive irrational   (D) negative irrational
(√3+2)² = 3 + 4√3 + 4 = 7 + 4√3
(√3−2)² = 3 − 4√3 + 4 = 7 − 4√3
Sum = 7 + 4√3 + 7 − 4√3 = 14 (irrational terms cancel!)
✅ Answer: (A) positive rational number (= 14)
20241 MarkMCQ
Let x = a²b³cⁿ and y = a³bᵐc². LCM of x and y is a³b⁴c³. Then m + n is: (A) 10   (B) 7   (C) 6   (D) 5
LCM takes highest powers: For c: max(n, 2) = 3 → n = 3
For b: max(3, m) = 4 → m = 4
m + n = 4 + 3 = 7
✅ Answer: (B) m + n = 7
20241 MarkMCQ
For any prime number p, if p divides a² (where a is real), then p also divides: (A) a   (B) a^(1/2)   (C) a^(3/2)   (D) a^(1/8)
This is a direct application of the theorem: If a prime p divides a², then p divides a.
This is the key theorem used in all irrationality proofs!
✅ Answer: (A) a
20243 MarksSA
(b) State true/false and justify: (i) 2×3×5×7 + 7 is composite. (ii) 2×3×5×7 + 1 is composite.
(i) 2×3×5×7 + 7 = 7(2×3×5 + 1) = 7 × 31.
Has factors 7 and 31 (other than 1 and itself) → TRUE, it is composite.

(ii) 2×3×5×7 + 1 = 210 + 1 = 211.
211 is a prime number → FALSE, it is NOT composite.

20241 MarkMCQ
√0.4 is a/an: (A) natural number   (B) integer   (C) rational number   (D) irrational number
√0.4 = √(4/10) = √(2/5) = √2/√5 = √10/5
√10 is irrational (10 is not a perfect square) → √10/5 is irrational.
✅ Answer: (D) irrational number
20241 MarkMCQ
Which of the following cannot be the unit digit of 8ⁿ (n is a natural number)? (A) 4   (B) 2   (C) 0   (D) 6
Powers of 8 → unit digits follow the cycle: 8¹→8, 8²→4, 8³→2, 8⁴→6, 8⁵→8...
Cycle = {8, 4, 2, 6}. The digit 0 never appears.
✅ Answer: (C) 0
20241 MarkA/R
Assertion (A): For any two prime numbers p and q, HCF is 1 and LCM is p+q.
Reason (R): For any two natural numbers, HCF × LCM = product of numbers.
Assertion (A): HCF of two distinct primes = 1 ✓. But LCM = p×q (NOT p+q) ✗ → A is FALSE
Reason (R): HCF × LCM = product of numbers is a standard theorem → R is TRUE
✅ Answer: (D) A is false but R is true.
20245 MarksLA
(b) Let p, q, r be distinct primes. Prove p·q·r + q is composite. Also give examples where p·q·r+1 is (i) composite (ii) prime.
Part 1 — Composite:
p·q·r + q = q(p·r + 1)
Since q > 1 and (p·r + 1) > 1, the expression has factors other than 1 and itself → composite ✓

📦 Case Study Questions

New CBSE Pattern Case Studies

These 4-5 mark integrated questions are now guaranteed in CBSE Boards!

CASE STUDY

🏫 Case Study 1: Science Competition

In a science competition, 60 students from Class 8, 84 students from Class 9, and 108 students from Class 10 participated. The school wanted to divide them into groups with equal number of students from each class.

(i) Find the total number of participants.
✅ Answer: 60+84+108 = 252
(ii) Find the LCM of 60, 84 and 108.
✅ Answer: 60=2²×3×5, 84=2²×3×7, 108=2²×3³ → LCM = 2²×3³×5×7 = 3780
(iii) Find the minimum number of rooms required.
✅ Answer: HCF(60,84,108) = 12. Rooms = 252/12 = 21 rooms
CASE STUDY

🎂 Case Study 2: Khushi's Birthday Party

Khushi wants to organize her birthday party. Being health conscious, she decided to serve only fruits. She bought 36 apples and 60 bananas and decided to distribute fruits equally among all guests. She later decided to add 42 mangoes.

(i) How many guests can Khushi invite at the most?
✅ Answer: HCF(36,60) = 12. She can invite at most 12 guests.
(ii) How many apples and bananas will each guest get?
✅ Answer: 36/12 = 3 apples, 60/12 = 5 bananas each.
(iii) If mangoes are also added, how many guests at most?
✅ Answer: HCF(36,60,42) = 6. At most 6 guests.
CASE STUDY

📚 Case Study 3: Prime Number Game (CBSE 2024)

Ms. Mukta planned a prime number game. She announced the number 2 and asked first student to multiply by a prime, pass to second who multiplied by a prime, and so on. The last student got 173250.

Prime factorisation: 173250 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 5 × 5 × 7 × 11 = 2 × 3² × 5³ × 7 × 11

(i) What is the least prime number used by students?
✅ Answer: 3 (since 2 was the starting number)
(ii)(a) How many students are in the class?
✅ Answer: 7 students (one for each prime factor after 2)
(ii)(b) What is the highest prime number used?
✅ Answer: 11
(iii) Which prime was used maximum times?
✅ Answer: 5 (used 3 times)
⚠️ Common Mistakes

Don't Lose Marks Over These!

❌ WRONG (Common Mistake)
"HCF(a,b,c) × LCM(a,b,c) = a × b × c"

Students apply the product formula for THREE numbers — this is INCORRECT!
✅ CORRECT
HCF × LCM = a × b only for TWO numbers.

For three numbers, calculate HCF and LCM separately using prime factorisation.
❌ WRONG
"√16 is irrational because it's a square root"

Students assume ALL square roots are irrational.
✅ CORRECT
√16 = 4, which is a natural number (rational).

√p is irrational only if p is a prime or a non-perfect-square integer.
❌ WRONG
"LCM of two primes p and q = p + q"

Very common error in Assertion-Reason type MCQs!
✅ CORRECT
LCM of two distinct primes p and q = p × q

HCF of two distinct primes = always 1
❌ WRONG
In irrationality proofs, students skip writing "let a = 2c" step and jump directly to contradiction — loses marks!
✅ CORRECT
Always show FULL STEPS: Assume rational → substitute a=pc → show p divides b → contradiction. Each step = marks!
❌ WRONG
"Sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational"

Example: Students think √2 + (–√2) = irrational
✅ CORRECT
√2 + (–√2) = 0 (rational!)

Sum of two irrationals may or may not be irrational. But rational + irrational = ALWAYS irrational.
⚠️
Board Exam Trap: The question "HCF of two consecutive even numbers" = 2, NOT 4. Students often write 4, but consecutive even numbers like 4 and 6 have HCF = 2, not 4.

🔢 Number Hierarchy

  • N ⊂ W ⊂ Z ⊂ Q ⊂ R
  • Q ∪ Irrational = R
  • Q ∩ Irrational = φ (empty)
  • 1 = neither prime nor composite
  • 2 = only even prime number
  • 4 = smallest composite number

⚙️ HCF & LCM Rules

  • HCF: lowest powers of common factors
  • LCM: highest powers of all factors
  • HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b (TWO only!)
  • HCF of primes = 1
  • LCM of primes = their product
  • HCF always divides LCM

📐 Irrationality Proof Template

  • Step 1: Assume rational = a/b (co-prime)
  • Step 2: Square both sides
  • Step 3: Show p divides a²
  • Step 4: ∴ p divides a → a = pc
  • Step 5: Show p divides b
  • Step 6: Contradiction → irrational ✓

🔄 Decimal Expansions

  • Terminating ↔ q = 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ ONLY
  • Non-terminating repeating ↔ other prime factors in q
  • Irrational → non-terminating non-repeating
  • 13/3125 terminates (3125 = 5⁵)
  • 1/7 = 0.142857̄ (repeating)
  • All rational → terminating OR NT repeating

⚡ Speed Formulas

  • Largest ÷ (a,b,c) leaving r₁,r₂,r₃ = HCF(a–r₁, b–r₂, c–r₃)
  • Smallest ÷ (a,b,c) leaving same r = LCM(a,b,c)+r
  • Zeros in N = min(pow of 2, pow of 5)
  • Numbers up to A div by b = ⌊A/b⌋
  • Total factors of N=aᵏbˡcᵐ = (k+1)(l+1)(m+1)

🧠 Key Identities

  • √p irrational for all primes p
  • rational ± irrational = irrational
  • nonzero rational × irrational = irrational
  • irrational ± irrational = may be rational
  • irrational × irrational = may be rational
  • 6ⁿ NEVER ends in 0 for any natural n
